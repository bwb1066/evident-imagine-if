The four-day discovery sequence

D1

Tuesday · Detection

During Priya's live FV5000 session on Layer 2/3 SST+ neurons, Lucia flagged ROI-83 as showing bursting at 3 Hz with bimodal IEI — different from the tonic ~8 Hz pattern of the surrounding cells. (Pillar 1A.) Lucia marked the slice for post-hoc imaging.

Priya time: 22 min (the live session question)

D2

Wednesday · Cross-reference

Lucia ran the same burst-feature detector across the lab's full SST-Cre image library — 84 slices, 4,247 cells from the prior 9 months. Surfaced 119 cells with the same bimodal IEI signature, distributed across 23 different slices from 4 different mice. This was the moment Priya realized the lab had been seeing this without seeing it.

Priya time: 14 min reviewing Lucia's output

D3

Thursday · Literature

Lucia found three relevant papers (Hu 2022, Naka 2024, Pfeffer 2023) suggesting a Martinotti-cell subtype with similar physiology — but no calcium-imaging characterization in this Cre line at this developmental window. Confirmation that this could be both novel and meaningful. Priya called her PI.

Priya time: 45 min · including the PI call

D4

Friday · Confirmation experiment

Priya ran a targeted experiment that morning: same FV5000 session protocol, but added post-hoc morphological imaging on the burst-firing cells specifically. 8 of 9 post-hoc imaged burst-cells showed Martinotti-like axonal morphology. The phenotype was real.

Priya time: 4 hrs · the experiment itself

LUCIA — why this would have been 9 months instead of 4 days

Pre-Lucia, this is what would have happened. Priya would have noticed something odd about ROI-83 during the live session and probably forgotten by the end of the day — there are dozens of small things to notice at every session. Even if she'd written it down, she would not have had time to manually re-check the 84-slice / 4,247-cell library to see whether the pattern was consistent. The novel population would have stayed hidden in plain sight across multiple papers, possibly until a competing lab published it first.

The 9-month estimate is conservative. A motivated postdoc could have done this analysis manually — 2-3 weeks per slice, 84 slices, on top of running normal experiments. Realistically the timeline is "next thesis student picks it up in 2 years" or "we see it in a competitor's paper and kick ourselves."

The 4-day timeline is honest. Lucia didn't make the discovery — Priya did. Lucia made it possible for Priya to investigate her own intuition fast enough to act on it before it faded. Detection in real time (pillar 1A), cross-referencing across the library (pillar 4B's normalization fabric in single-lab mode), literature triangulation (pillar 3A's citation work), confirmation experiment (pillar 2A's protocol drafting). Every transformation in the deck contributed.
